    Calibrated probabilities: the machine-learning layer

    oracle3.ml learns how resolved Kalshi and Polymarket contracts actually turned out given what was observable while they traded, and serves that model to agents through the model_probability MCP tool. The benchmark compares four learners against the market price and the probit-shift model of Yang (2026), out of sample.

    Results: evidence/ml_calibration/REPORT.md (machine-readable: results.json in the same directory).

    Data

    python -m oracle3.ml.collect kalshi|polymarket --out DIR downloads resolved contracts and their price histories into a resumable cache. Every request is a read-only GET; Kalshi requests are signed when KALSHI_API_KEY_ID and KALSHI_PRIVATE_KEY_PATH are set (or given in --env-file), which only raises the rate limit.

    • Kalshi: settled events from each series' public event listing whose date falls in the window, up to 200 per series spread evenly over time and at most 20,000 per category; up to four markets per event, chosen by a seeded hash of the ticker. Events from before Kalshi moved to KX tickers are not in the public listing (they stay in an archive that can be read by exact ticker but not listed), and most current series only began in late 2025 or 2026. On both venues most sampled events resolve in 2026, when far more were listed. Bars are the venue's candlesticks (best bid and ask, last trade, volume): one-minute bars for contracts scheduled to live up to two days, hourly up to 120 days, daily beyond.
    • Polymarket: closed events from the Gamma API, sampled the same way; binary markets with a clean 1/0 payout. Price history comes from the Data API v2 at its finest permanent grain (3 hours), so contracts scheduled to live less than a day, and the auto-generated "Up or Down" series, are left out. Polymarket publishes no time-stamped spread or volume history.

    Contracts are chosen before any price or outcome is looked at; lifetime volume, final prices and results play no part in the sample.

    The defaults reproduce the published sample: scheduled ends from 2024-01-01 to 2026-11-05. The cache is gzipped JSON, and a later run with other settings reuses whatever is already cached. Each run names its sample after its window and caps and writes a selection.json; the dataset is built from that selection only, never from everything that happens to be in the cache.

    Snapshots and features

    python -m oracle3.ml.dataset --cache DIR --out snapshots.parquet makes one row per contract at 25%, 50% and 75% of its scheduled life, kept only if the market was still trading then. Each row's features use bars completed by the prediction time and nothing later:

    Feature Meaning
    price, logit_price, extremity displayed price: bid/ask midpoint if the spread is at most 10¢, else last trade
    log_hours_to_end, log_life_hours, elapsed_fraction time left until and since the scheduled end and open
    dp_life05, dp_life20, dp_1h, dp_24h price change over the last 5% and 20% of the scheduled life, 1 hour and 24 hours
    vol_logit_life20, hours_since_change volatility of log-odds changes, hours since the price last moved
    spread, log_volume_to_date, log_volume_life10, active_share_life10 Kalshi only: spread, traded volume to date and recently, share of recent bars with trades
    is_kalshi, cat_* venue and one of nine shared categories

    Leakage controls, each covered by a test in tests/test_ml_layer.py: a bar counts only after it has ended; appending extreme future bars changes no feature; time features use the scheduled end, not the actual close (which moves earlier when an outcome happens early); Polymarket's settlement tick, which the API appends to resolved series, is removed.

    Validation

    python -m oracle3.ml.benchmark --snapshots S.parquet --evidence DIR --bundle DIR (install oracle3[ml-bench], which adds XGBoost and CatBoost to the base oracle3[ml] extra)

    • Event-grouped, purged walk-forward cross-validation. Events are ordered by their first prediction time and cut into consecutive blocks; each fold tests on the next block and trains only on events whose outcomes were known at least 24 hours before it begins. An event's contracts never sit on both sides.
    • Tuning on training data only. Inside each training window, three purged inner walk-forward blocks are each predicted by models fitted on earlier training events; hyperparameters are chosen by the Brier score of those out-of-fold predictions, and LightGBM early-stops on them.
    • Isotonic recalibration is fitted to the same inner out-of-fold predictions and kept only if it passes a check on training data: fitted on the earlier inner blocks, it must lower the Brier score of the later ones. The model refitted on the whole window is stronger than the inner models, and a map that only suits the weaker ones would distort it; the report shows both variants.
    • Learners (eight): L2-penalized logistic regression; a spline GAM (penalized logistic regression on cubic-spline bases, one smooth curve per feature); random forest; LightGBM; LightGBM constrained to be monotone in the market price; XGBoost; CatBoost; a two-layer MLP. Ensembles: the mean of the eight learners' probabilities, and a logistic regression stacked on their log-odds and fitted to the inner out-of-fold predictions. Baselines: the market price; the market price recalibrated by isotonic regression alone; and the paper's model (WangMLE), both one λ per venue and the hierarchical form with the paper's covariates.
    • Scores are computed on test folds only: Brier score, log loss and expected calibration error (10 equal-width bins), with 95% intervals from resampling whole events.

    Serving

    The best learner whose library is in the base oracle3[ml] install (everything except XGBoost and CatBoost) is refitted on all snapshots in the same way and saved with its recalibration map and a manifest of features, hyperparameters, data range, out-of-sample scores and file hashes (checked on load). model_probability(venue, market_id) rebuilds the features for an open market from public data at call time and returns the model probability next to the market price:

    {"model_probability": 0.231, "market_price": 0.25, "model_minus_market": -0.019,
 "model": "...", "out_of_sample": {"vs_market": {"brier_reduction_pct": "..."}}, "features": {"...": "..."}}

    Install the extra with pip install 'oracle3[ml]'. Set ORACLE3_ML_BUNDLE to serve a bundle you trained yourself.

    The output is a calibrated probability estimate. It does not account for fees, spreads, depth or resolution-rule details, and a better-calibrated probability is not by itself a profitable trade.
